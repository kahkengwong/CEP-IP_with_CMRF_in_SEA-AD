==========================================================================
  G3 — PRE-SPECIFIED shape test: linear, or flat-then-rising?
==========================================================================
  THE CLAIM BEING PRE-REGISTERED
    An ambient or degradation artifact should scale smoothly with
    pathology if it scales at all. A biological threshold effect should
    look flat across the low stages and then rise. This section asks
    which shape the DONOR-LEVEL data show, and records the answer now,
    before Part 11c is run, so that agreement later is a confirmation
    rather than a result the analysis was steered toward.

  WHAT IS ACTUALLY FITTED
    For each response y and pathology axis s (an integer stage):
      linear : y ~ rank(s)
      step   : y ~ 1[s > cut], best cut chosen over the interior stages
    and the two are compared by R2. A step that beats the linear fit by
    a clear margin is the flat-then-rising shape. Both are also refitted
    on tissue-quality residuals, because degradation is the competing
    explanation for any pathology relationship here.

  LIMITS - carry these into any use of this section
    - n is at most 84 donors spread over 4-7 stages, so the per-stage
      cells are small and the cut point is weakly identified.
    - the step model has one more effective parameter than the linear
      one, so a small R2 gain is expected even under a linear truth.
    - the pathology axes are heavily correlated with each other, so the
      four tests are not four independent looks.
    - this is NOT the Part 11c Pre-IP/Post-IP test. See the header.
